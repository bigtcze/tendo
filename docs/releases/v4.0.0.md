Tendo 4.0 gives every screen a new, calmer look, adds an **Admin center** for household administrators, makes signing in with an identity provider such as Pocket ID work for people who do not have a Tendo account yet, and simplifies the Docker Compose deployment to two containers. Your data is kept. 4.0 changes the database, so read [Updating from 3.x](#updating-from-3x) before you update.

## What looks different

- **A new look on every screen.** Tendo now uses the Inter typeface, served by Tendo itself (no font service is contacted), with a cool off-white background, dark navy text and a petrol-teal colour for main actions. Status colours are coral for **Needs attention**, cyan for **In progress**, amber for **Waiting**, and green-teal for **Coming up**. Status is still always written out, not shown by colour alone. Sign-in, setup, Account, Members, People and things, All items, item details, forms and error screens all follow the same design.
- **Home.** A top bar shows Tendo and who is signed in. Below the household name, status buttons show how many items need attention, are in progress, are waiting, or are coming up, and jump to that group; they appear when at least two groups have items. Items are listed in compact rows: the title, who or what it is for, the date and who is looking after it, and **Done**.
- **Phones.** The status buttons form one row you can scroll sideways, and **Done** is a check button at the end of each row. The bottom bar keeps exactly five destinations: Home, Items, People, Members, Account.
- **Light and dark.** Both appearances were redesigned with the new colours and their contrast was checked again. Tendo still follows your device setting unless you choose **Light** or **Dark** in **Account**, and it still applies the right appearance before the page is drawn.

## Household administrators

- **Owners are now administrators.** The update turns every household owner into an **administrator**, with the same abilities as before: inviting people and managing invitations. Members stay members.
- **Admin center.** Administrators find it at the end of the sidebar on a computer, and under **Account** → **Open Admin center** on any screen. It lists everyone in the household with their role and how they sign in (password, single sign-on, or both).
- **Change roles.** **Make administrator** and **Make member** change a person's role after a confirmation. A household always keeps at least one administrator: Tendo refuses to demote the last one. Every change is checked on the server, not only hidden in the interface, and is recorded.
- **Password reset links.** An administrator can create a one-time **Reset password** link for a member who signs in with a password, and send it privately. The link works once, expires after 24 hours, is replaced when a new one is created, and can be cancelled. Using it sets a new password and signs that person out on every device. Tendo sends no email: you share the link yourself. The administrator never sees or chooses the new password, but anyone who has the link can set it, so send it privately. Only a hash of each link is stored. See [Reset a password](https://github.com/bigtcze/tendo/blob/v4.0.0/docs/user/household-members.md#reset-a-password-administrators).

## Signing in with an identity provider

If you use OIDC, for example Pocket ID ([setup](https://github.com/bigtcze/tendo/blob/v4.0.0/docs/admin/oidc.md)):

- **New people can sign in with it.** The first sign-in with the provider no longer requires an existing Tendo account. Tendo shows **Finish signing in**, where the person chooses **Create a new Tendo account** or **Use my existing account**. A new account has no password and, without an invitation, opens **Join your household by invitation**. On an invite page, **Continue with …** carries the invitation through sign-in, and finishing accepts it if it is still valid. A provider sign-in never adds anyone to a household without an invitation and never makes anyone an administrator; invitations always add people as members.
- **Existing accounts connect themselves once.** The first time someone with a Tendo password uses the provider, Tendo asks whether to use their existing account, confirmed with their Tendo password once, or to create a new one. Accounts from 3.0 have no saved email address, so this one confirmation is how they are recognised from then on.
- **Recognised by verified email.** Tendo now asks the provider for the person's email address. It connects a sign-in to an account automatically only when the provider marks the address as verified, exactly one account already trusts that address from the same provider, and that account has no other sign-in from that provider. Otherwise Tendo asks the person to finish signing in, or explains the conflict. A missing, unverified or conflicting address never connects accounts, and a name alone never connects anything. A sign-in that is already connected keeps working even if its email address changes at the provider.
- **No more connect step in Account.** The **Connect …** button on **Your account** is gone. Sign-ins connected in 3.0 keep working unchanged, and password sign-in keeps working even when the provider is down.

Configure the provider to verify email addresses before it releases them. Tendo now requests the `openid` and `email` scopes.

## People and things

- **Members are people.** Everyone who belongs to the household now appears in **People and things** as a person, marked **Household member**, and can be chosen as who an item is for, in filters and in item forms. You no longer need a second, separate person entry for yourself or another member. Their name starts as their login and can be changed; they cannot be archived while they are a member.
- **Existing entries.** If a 3.0 person entry was linked to a member's account, that entry becomes the member's person, keeps its items and history, and returns to the active list if it was archived. Members without such an entry get a new one named after their login. If several entries were linked to the same member, the oldest one is used and the others stay as ordinary people with all their items. Nothing is deleted or merged, and nothing is matched by name. Anyone in the household can later choose **This is a household member…** in an older person entry's **Edit** dialog to make it a member's person on purpose ([how](https://github.com/bigtcze/tendo/blob/v4.0.0/docs/user/people-and-things.md)).

## Members and invitations

- Expired invitations no longer appear in the **Invitations** list or its pages. They still cannot be used, and Tendo keeps their record. Pending, accepted and revoked invitations show as before.

## Deployment: two containers instead of three

The published `compose.yaml` now has exactly two services, `postgres` and `app`. The separate one-shot `migrate` container and the inline `configs` block are gone. On every start the app itself creates or checks its restricted `tendo` database role, applies any pending database migrations, closes that privileged connection, and only then opens HTTP and reports healthy. Normal requests still use only the restricted role. The app refuses to start on a database that is partly migrated, newer than it supports, or whose `tendo` role has extra privileges.

**Security tradeoff.** Because the long-running app container now does this startup work, it keeps the PostgreSQL administrator password in its environment for as long as it runs, even after the privileged connection is closed. Anyone who can run `docker inspect` on it, or run code inside it, can read that password. In 3.0 the app container did not hold this password; only PostgreSQL and the short-lived `migrate` container did. This is a deliberate reduction in isolation in exchange for a simpler installation; protect access to the Docker daemon and the app container accordingly. Details: [configuration](https://github.com/bigtcze/tendo/blob/v4.0.0/docs/admin/configuration.md).

No new setting is needed: Compose builds the administrator connection from the `POSTGRES_PASSWORD` you already have.

## Updating from 3.x

1. [Back up](https://github.com/bigtcze/tendo/blob/v4.0.0/docs/admin/backup-restore.md#back-up) first. 4.0 changes the database schema, so going back to 3.0 needs that backup.
2. Download the 4.0 `compose.yaml` and set `TENDO_VERSION` in `.env` to `v4` or `latest`. `v3` and `v3.0` stay on 3.0.0 and never install 4.0.
3. Run `docker compose pull`, then `docker compose up -d --wait --remove-orphans`. `--remove-orphans` removes the old `migrate` container. Large households take longer to migrate; the app shows as starting until it is done.

Full steps: [Updating from 3.x to 4.0](https://github.com/bigtcze/tendo/blob/v4.0.0/docs/admin/update.md#updating-from-3x-to-40). From 2.x or 1.x, read the earlier sections of the same guide first; you can then update straight to 4.0.

What the update keeps: accounts, passwords, signed-in sessions, connected identity-provider sign-ins, households, people and things, items, completion history, and invitations. Owners become administrators. Members get their own person entry in **People and things** as described above.

To go back to 3.0.0 after updating, [restore the backup](https://github.com/bigtcze/tendo/blob/v4.0.0/docs/admin/backup-restore.md#restore-onto-a-fresh-installation) you took before updating into a fresh 3.0.0 installation. Changes made after the backup are lost.

If you restore a backup into a fresh 4.0 installation, the steps changed: run `docker compose run --rm --no-deps app ensure-role` before restoring. See [back up and restore](https://github.com/bigtcze/tendo/blob/v4.0.0/docs/admin/backup-restore.md).

## For API clients

4.0 is a new major version because some `/api/v1` values changed:

- The household role `owner` is now `admin` everywhere (`MemberRole` is `admin` or `member`). Invitation routes still answer members with the old code `403 owner_required`; the new account routes use `403 admin_required`.
- Every member has one canonical linked person. PATCH cannot archive it, unlink or relink it, or change its type away from `person` (`409 member_person_locked`). Creating or linking another subject with that member's `linkedUserId` returns `409 member_person_exists`; use `PUT /households/{id}/members/{userId}/person` to reassociate on purpose.
- New: household accounts and roles (`/households/{id}/accounts`), password reset links, member persons (`/households/{id}/members/{userId}/person`), first OIDC sign-in (`/auth/oidc/pending`), `viewerRole` on the household, and `includeExpired` on the invitation list (default `true`, so existing clients see every invitation as before).
- The OIDC login callback can now redirect to `/login#oidc=continue` without creating a session; the client then finishes account creation or password confirmation through the `/auth/oidc/pending` endpoints. The callback no longer returns `identity_not_linked`. Deprecated but unchanged: `GET /auth/oidc/identity` and the `link` purpose of `POST /auth/oidc/start`.

`api/openapi.yaml` and the [API conventions](https://github.com/bigtcze/tendo/blob/v4.0.0/docs/development/api.md) describe each change.

## Testing and quality

- Every existing feature is still covered by tests. Automated browser tests run the production build against a real PostgreSQL database at 320 to 1440 pixels wide, in English and Czech, light and dark, with 200 % and 400 % zoom, keyboard use and households with more than 1000 items. They also check text size, touch targets, contrast and that no page scrolls sideways.
- Database tests cover each new migration on real data, including owners becoming administrators, member persons, saved email addresses and reset links. Security tests cover administrator-only access, the last administrator, other households, reset-link reuse, expiry and cancellation, and identity-provider sign-in with unverified, changed, duplicated and conflicting email addresses and simultaneous sign-ins.
- Deployment tests start the two-container stack from scratch, restart it, update real data from the published 1.0.0, 2.1.0 and 3.0.0 releases, and check that the app refuses to start, without reporting ready, when PostgreSQL is unavailable, the migration record is dirty or newer than the app, a database password is wrong, or the database role has extra privileges.
- The screenshots in this note and the README come from the running app with sample data. The new look was compared side by side with the approved design drawings; the review is recorded in the [V4 qualification record](https://github.com/bigtcze/tendo/blob/v4.0.0/docs/design/v4-qualification.md).
- Reviews were done by AI reviewers. No household members, screen-reader users or penetration testers have tested this release, and none of these checks is a formal security or accessibility certification.

## Known limitations

- **The only administrator cannot recover a forgotten password.** Administrators cannot reset their own password, and **Account** has no password change. If a household has a single administrator, make a second person administrator, or keep the password in a password manager. See [household members](https://github.com/bigtcze/tendo/blob/v4.0.0/docs/user/household-members.md#reset-a-password-administrators).
- The app container keeps the PostgreSQL administrator password in its environment (see the security tradeoff above).
- An account created by mistake through the identity provider can be removed only while it has no household. Tendo does not merge accounts, and this release cannot remove a member from a household.
- A password reset link that was still usable when a backup was taken works again after that backup is restored, until it expires.
- Dark appearance needs Chrome or Edge 123, Firefox 120, Safari 17.5, or newer. Older browsers show the light appearance. The appearance choice is saved in each browser, not in your account.

## Not included

- Notifications or reminders outside the app.
- Email: Tendo sends no invitations or reset links by email.
- More than one household per account in the interface.
- TLS. Tendo serves HTTP and expects a reverse proxy in front of it.
- Running under a URL path prefix.
